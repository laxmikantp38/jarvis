Who is on the floor

What the floor tells you

The biggest room is yours

Room size encodes authority. Yours is largest, Jarvis's is next, workers get cabins. The floor plan states the permission hierarchy without a word of copy.

Approvals physically arrive

A held task travels from the worker's cabin to your desk and sits there glowing until you act. Work that needs you is impossible to miss.

An empty cabin means work is moving

Workers stay seated unless assigned. A figure in the corridor always means a task just changed hands.

Jarvis never leaves its office

The orchestrator delegates; it does not execute, and it cannot approve on your behalf. Its stillness while the floor moves is the architecture made literal.

Light does the modelling

Ceiling pools, gradient walls, contact shadows and monitor spill give the room depth. Screen glow tints whoever is sitting in front of it.

Motion is optional

Honours reduced-motion and has an off switch. Every state stays readable from posture, colour and the feed.
