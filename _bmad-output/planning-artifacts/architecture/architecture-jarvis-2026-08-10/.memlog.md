- (decision by coach) PROPOSED: satisfy the screen requirement in two stages. Stage 1 - ON-DEMAND SCREENSHOT over the private network, triggered from the phone, cheap in bandwidth and trivial to implement, and probably meets the actual need. Stage 2 - live stream via WebRTC peer-to-peer over Tailscale only if the screenshot proves insufficient. Never relay video through the Rs 1500/month VPS; that budget cannot carry continuous video.
- (decision by user) DEPLOYMENT TOPOLOGY DECIDED BY USER: full system runs LOCAL. A SMALL RELAY COMPONENT runs on the server - it serves a mobile web UI, displays the latest screenshot, and accepts commands. The local agent DIALS OUT to the relay to collect commands and push screenshots, so no inbound port is ever opened on the local machine. WhatsApp and Telegram follow the same path. This resolves the earlier reachability-vs-no-inbound-ports tension.
- (decision by coach) GOVERNING INVARIANT for the relay: the relay is a DUMB, ZERO-KNOWLEDGE COURIER with NO AUTHORITY. It transports opaque encrypted envelopes and holds no plaintext, no credentials to the local machine, and no permission-granting power. Every permission decision, and every execution, happens on the local machine. A fully compromised relay must be able to do nothing except deny service.
- (constraint) SCREENSHOT EXPOSURE: screenshots of his desktop would otherwise sit in plaintext on a rented VPS and may contain Gate6 or client content. Required mitigations - end-to-end encryption with a key held only by the local machine and his phone (relay stores ciphertext only), latest-screenshot-only retention with a short TTL and auto-delete, and configurable capture blackout windows (e.g. during Gate6 working hours).
- (decision) L3 remains local-UI-only and is NOT grantable from the relay, mobile web, WhatsApp or Telegram. A compromised phone or chat channel must never be able to authorise a destructive action. L0-L2 are commandable remotely, subject to the normal permission model evaluated locally.
- (decision) PARADIGM: hexagonal (ports and adapters) around a deterministic core. Chosen because every volatile requirement in this system sits at an edge - swappable database, swappable model provider, multiplying channels, and a deployment that moves from local to local-plus-relay. Each becomes an adapter change rather than a redesign.
- (decision) AD-3 resolves the pluggable-DB tension: the RELATIONAL schema is the canonical model, and any non-relational adapter must enforce the same invariants itself (goal-tree acyclicity, task.project_id present, no action without a resolved permission decision, append-only logs). An adapter that cannot enforce an invariant is non-compliant. This preserves the data model instead of degrading it to a lowest common denominator to fit Mongo.
